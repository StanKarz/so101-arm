Two arms, two voltages

20 minshort
Failures 2

Each arm is its own circuit: a brick, an adapter board and six motors in a chain. USB from the Mac powers only the board's serial chip, so with the brick off a script fails with Motor 'gripper' was not found and the fault looks like software.

12 V brick5 A since 14 Nov→Follower boardF · 12V …5B7B0168461→6 × C04712 V, 1:345USB → Mac: data only
5 V brickleader only→Leader boardL · 5V …5B7B0093921→C001 · 2 × C044 · 3 × C0467.4 V motors on 5 VUSB → Mac: data only

Voltage has to match the motor, and the mistake is only dangerous one way: 12 V motors on 5 V are just weak, 7.4 V motors on 12 V burn. The leader's motors run slow and weak on 5 V, which is fine for something you push by hand.

Between the rotor and the horn sits a gearbox of ratio \(N\). Driving it, output torque rises by \(N\) and speed falls by \(N\). Pushing it from the horn, you spin the rotor \(N\) times faster, so the rotor's inertia reaches your hand multiplied by \(N^2\):

\[ J_{\text{felt at horn}} = J_{\text{load}} + N^2 J_{\text{rotor}} \]

Friction in the gear train grows roughly with \(N\) too. From 1:147 to 1:345 that's about 2.3× the friction and 5.5× the inertia. Hence the leader's mix: 1:345 on shoulder_lift so the arm holds up its own weight, 1:191 on pan and elbow, 1:147 on the wrist and gripper where your fingers do the moving. Failure 2 was a 12 V 1:345 in the leader's gripper.

Motor

TryDrag the horn on C046, then make the same movement on C047. The rotor count is why a 1:345 leader gripper feels wrong. Then look at the encoder line: same counts per horn turn on every variant, because the encoder reads the output shaft.

The bars are ratios against C046, from \(N\) and \(N^2\). The drag feel exaggerates them so you can notice. inspect_motors.py tells the voltage variants apart by their registers (Max_Voltage_Limit 140, 80 or 120), and gear ratio can't be read from a register at all, so C044 and C046 stay indistinguishable without the sticker.

2

One wire, six listeners

30 minshort
Failures 31011

A ping to ID 4 is six bytes on the white wire: FF FF 04 02 01 F8. That's the header, the ID, a length, the instruction, and a checksum (the inverted low byte of 04 + 02 + 01). At 1 Mbaud and 10 bits per byte it takes 60 µs. A sync read of all six positions is a 14-byte request and six 8-byte replies, 62 bytes, about 0.6 ms on the wire. A 60 Hz teleop loop has 16.7 ms per tick, so the bus has room to spare. You measured 200 of 200 sync reads clean at 11.8–12.0 V.

The ID is the only address, and every new motor ships as ID 1, which is why setup wants one motor on the board at a time and why failure 3 had you reaching for buried cables. The two sockets on a motor are joined inside the case, so there's no in or out. Anything that breaks the chain removes every motor after it, so which IDs answer tells you where to look.

3

The shape of the arm

35 minshort

The official model is a URDF, an XML file of links (mesh, mass, centre of mass) and joints (origin, axis, limits). Each joint's motor is bolted into the parent link and its horn drives the child. Forward kinematics is that chain multiplied out, one fixed transform and one rotation per joint:

\[ T_{\text{tool}}(q) = \prod_{i} T_{\text{origin},i}\; R_{a_i}(q_i) \]

At \(q = 0\), the middle of every joint's range, the arm stands in an L with the tool 353 mm out from the pan axis and 229 mm above the bench. Joints 2, 3 and 4 have parallel axes, so they all bend in one plane, and joint 1 turns that plane. Five joints place and aim the hand, and joint 6 only closes it.

Gravity load comes from the same chain. For joint \(j\) with axis \(a_j\) at \(o_j\), sum over every link \(i\) beyond it, with centre of mass \(c_i\):

\[ \tau_j = a_j \cdot \sum_{i > j} (c_i - o_j) \times m_i\, g \]

That's what the bars under the arm compute, live, from the URDF's masses (667 g in total).

Pose

Meshes and masses: TheRobotStudio/SO-ARM100 (Apache-2.0), decimated to about 160k faces and coloured to match your white PLA. Slider limits are the URDF's. "raw" uses your follower.json: the middle of the recorded range plus \(q \cdot 4095/360\), assuming the URDF and the motor agree on direction. Stall torque (30 kg·cm) and stall current (2.7 A) are retailer figures, and the current figure assumes current scales with torque, so read it as an estimate. It's static holding only: accelerating and gripping both add to it.

Simulation 3

DOF is the exponent

Each joint chooses its position independently of the others. If every joint can sit at k positions and there are n joints, the arm has k × k × … × k = kⁿ configurations. The number of DOF is n, the dimension of the space. Real joints are continuous, so the true count is infinite; k is whatever resolution you choose to count at.

Simulation 4

More joints than the task needs

A flat arm reaching a point on a page has a 2-DOF task: x and y. With two joints there are exactly two answers, elbow up or elbow down. Add a third joint and the hand can stay put while the arm sweeps through a whole family of poses. That leftover freedom is the null space. A 7-DOF arm doing a 6-DOF task (full position and orientation) has one spare DOF in the same way: the elbow can swing round while the hand stays fixed. Drag the target.

Calibration, and what it writes

20 minshort
Failures 4567

Calibration has two steps. Press Enter at the mid pose and LeRobot writes a homing offset so that pose reads 2047. Sweep every joint and it keeps the lowest and highest counts it sees. From then on every reading is reported relative to the middle of that range:

\[ q = \left(r - \frac{r_{\min} + r_{\max}}{2}\right) \cdot \frac{360}{4095} \]

This is why two arms with different motors can share one policy: \(q = 0\) means the middle of the travel on both. The failures all came from those two steps. A 17-second sweep that didn't reach the stops gave ranges of 5–430 ticks. Folding the arm at the mid pose pushed the sweep over the seam. Eyeballing the middle wasn't accurate enough. A stray Enter in the stdin buffer ended range recording instantly.

The encoder seam

The encoder counts 0 to 4095 round one turn, then starts again at 0. Calibration's first step writes a homing offset so that the pose you hold when you press Enter reads 2047, half a turn from the seam. Teleop is two control loops

20 minshort

The outer loop runs on the Mac at 60 Hz by default: sync-read the leader, convert to degrees, sync-write those as the follower's Goal_Position. The inner loop runs inside each STS3215, much faster, driving the motor towards the latest goal. LeRobot writes its gains when the follower connects: P = 16, I = 0, D = 32.

\[ \tau = K_p (q_{\text{goal}} - q) - K_d \dot q, \qquad \text{at rest with gravity:}\quad q_{\text{goal}} - q = \frac{\tau_g(q)}{K_p} \]

With \(K_i = 0\) nothing integrates that error away, so the follower settles short of the goal by an amount set by gravity and P. Your first run on 29 Oct lasted 80 s with Status 0 on every motor afterwards, the follower at 32–38 °C with the gripper warmest and the leader at 24–27 °C.

This matters for learning because lerobot-record stores both sides for every frame. action is the leader and observation.state is the follower, and the gap between them is real signal: the policy learns to command the leader's positions, sag and lag included.

What the cameras will see

20 minshort
Failures 1920

Both cameras are plain UVC devices, so there's no driver and OpenCV opens them by index. On macOS that index is the position after sorting every camera's uniqueID as text, which is why an iPhone drifting into range renumbered everything and the "wrist" photo came out as your face. camera_index.py --export looks them up by name. The wrist camera delivers 640×480 at 30.3 fps and the Insta360 640×360 at 29.9 fps, and those exact modes are what check_camera.py tests.

The ET-S231's 120° is the diagonal. For a 4:3 sensor under a pinhole model, the vertical field of view follows from \(\tan(\theta_v/2) = \tan(\theta_d/2) \cdot \tfrac{3}{5}\), which gives about 92°. A real wide lens bends the edges, so treat it as a first guess.

A camera view is also a token budget. ACT's ResNet18 reduces every 32 × 32 pixel patch to one token, so a 25 mm block that covers 30 pixels is about one token out of 240. That's the question this sim is for: from the positions you plan to use, how much of the policy's input is the thing you want picked up?

7

Printed parts are approximate

10 minshort
Failures 131621

FDM PLA comes out slightly off the CAD, and the wrist-cam mount is a third-party part whose README says it "still requires adaption to the new camera". Three fits needed hand work on one bracket: the hole pitch (27.0 mm against about 28 mm), the connector notch, and the flap in its slot. Slicing the STL with trimesh and fitting Hough circles to photos gave you the 27.0 against 28 measurement, which is a useful trick: CAD files can answer "why doesn't this fit" before you cut anything.

Two rules came out of it. Check the holes line up before removing any plastic. Take material off the cheap part (the mount), never the structural one (the housing).

Screw length comes down to a stack you can measure. Push an M3×10 through with no nut, and depth-rod the tip past the inner wall to get protrusion \(p\). The nut's seat depth is then \(T = 10 - p\), and the screw has to land in \([T - 0.9,\ T]\): long enough to catch the nut, short enough not to foul the motor. The README's order is what makes this work. Nuts flush in the recesses, motor 6 back in so it backs the nuts, M3×8 last from outside. With the motor out, the screw just pushes the nut away, which is why 8 mm looked too short on 31 Oct. Done in that order, the flap bolted on 14 Nov.

TryOpen the 3D arm's Wrist view and find the mount's flap and motor 6 behind it. Then look at wrist_roll: the camera rides on the far side of that joint, so the wrist view rolls with the gripper while the scene view doesn't.

What EEPROM is, and the rule

EEPROM (electrically erasable programmable read-only memory) keeps its contents with the power off. It is slow to write and wears out after a limited number of writes, typically rated around 100,000. RAM is fast and never wears, but forgets everything when power goes. The servo's microcontroller copies its EEPROM settings into working memory at power-on. On the STS3215 everything below address 40 is EEPROM and Torque_Enable at 40 is the first RAM register, which is why a motor powers up limp. The Lock register guards EEPROM; LeRobot opens it whenever torque is off, so its writes to P, I, D and the gripper limits on every connect persist after it exits.

From LeRobot 0.7.0 the table lives in the Rust driver, not in a Python file. Print the whole thing with uv run python -c "import rustypot; [print(r.addr, r.size, r.name) for r in rustypot.Sts3215PyController.registers()]". The EEPROM/RAM split comes from Feetech's memory table; the driver doesn't record it.

Answer in your own words
  • With the dead socket, why did ID 1 answer from one socket while nothing answered from the other? Draw the bus as it was wired.the fault-finder and failure 11
    Answer

    The two sockets are joined inside the case, so a dead socket only cuts off whatever is plugged into it. Board cable in the good socket: motor 1 has power and answers, but the cable on to motors 2–6 leaves through the dead socket, so they're silent. Board cable in the dead socket: motor 1 gets no power, and everything else hangs off motor 1, so nothing answers. Drawn: board → [good | dead] shoulder_pan → shoulder_lift → … → gripper.

  • Your elbow has 195° of travel. How far from the middle can you hold it when you press Enter before range recording wraps?the encoder seam, chapter 4
    Answer

    About 82°. Calibration makes the held pose read 2047, half a turn (180°) from the seam. The travel reaches 97.5° either side of its true middle, so holding δ off-centre puts one end 97.5° + |δ| from the held pose, and that has to stay under 180°: |δ| < 82.5°. The table's 81° is the same sum on your measured offsets.

  • Why does the follower trail the leader slightly in teleop? Name two settings from this week that cause it.the teleop sim and the glossary: PID, max_relative_target
    Answer

    P = 16 with I = 0: with no integral term the follower settles short of the goal by the gravity torque over the gain, and LeRobot softens P to cut jitter, which widens that gap. D = 32 resists velocity, so fast moves fall further behind. On top of that the goal only updates at 60 Hz, and until 14 Nov the 2 A brick sagged on fast moves. max_relative_target would add lag if you set it; it's off by default.

  • Why can't the gripper approach a block from the side, out of the arm's plane?the 3D arm, arm plane on
    Answer

    Joints 2, 3 and 4 have parallel axes, so they all move the hand within one vertical plane, and shoulder_pan only turns that plane. wrist_roll spins the hand about its own approach axis, which stays in the plane. With five arm joints nothing can tip the approach direction out of the plane; that would take a sixth, a wrist yaw.

  • In the "Reach forward" pose, which joint carries the most load and why? What does that say about the leader's motor choice?the load bars and chapter 1
    Answer

    shoulder_lift. Its axis is horizontal and every link beyond it hangs off it at the longest lever arm, so \(\tau_j\) is largest there, with the elbow second. shoulder_pan's axis is vertical, so gravity barely loads it. That's why the leader's only 1:345 motor sits at shoulder_lift: the high ratio's friction holds the leader up when you let go, while the wrist joints carry almost nothing and get 1:147 so they stay light under your fingers.

  • What does the tutorial say limits dynamics-based control, and which of those limits shows up when your gripper closes on a block?tutorial §2.4
    Answer

    Four limits: chaining separate modules (sensing, planning, IK, control) is brittle and errors compound across them; every new task or sensor needs custom engineering, so it doesn't scale; analytical models of contact, friction and compliance are approximations; and the approach can't use the growing pool of robot data. The third shows up at your gripper: how a PLA jaw grips and slips on a block is what a hand-written model gets wrong. The tutorial's own figure uses friction at the gripper as its example.

  • In a LeRobotDataset, where does a camera frame live, and where does a joint position live?tutorial §1.1
    Answer

    Joint positions are rows in Parquet tables, one row per frame, with observation.state (follower) and action (leader) next to the timestamp, frame index and episode index. Camera frames are encoded into MP4 files, grouped by camera, with several episodes per file in format v3.0. JSON metadata ties the two together: feature schema, fps, normalisation statistics and where each episode starts and ends.

Answer in your own words
  • Why does predicting k actions at once reduce compounding error? Put it in terms of how many times the policy is queried over one episode.ACT, method section
    Answer

    An episode of T steps queried every step means T decisions, each a chance to add an error the next one starts from. With chunks of k the policy is queried T/k times, so the effective horizon shrinks k-fold. At 30 fps and k = 100, a 20 s episode is 6 queries instead of 600.

  • What does the CVAE's latent z capture during training, and what is z set to at test time?ACT, method section
    Answer

    The style of that particular demonstration: whatever varies between human demos that the observation doesn't explain, like speed or a hesitation. The encoder sees the joint state and the true action chunk and outputs z. At test time the encoder is dropped and z = 0, the prior's mean, so the policy acts deterministically.

  • What does temporal ensembling average, and how are older predictions weighted against newer ones?ACT, method section
    Answer

    Every chunk predicted in the last k steps contains an action for the current timestep, and temporal ensembling averages them with weights \(w_i = e^{-m i}\), where \(w_0\) is the oldest. So older predictions weigh more, and a smaller m lets new observations in faster. It needs a query every step, which is the 41 ms against 33 ms problem in the explorer's note.

  • What happens in the ablations when the chunk size drops to 1?ACT, ablations
    Answer

    Success collapses. At k = 1 ACT is a one-step policy and the benefit of chunking disappears; the chunk-size plot climbs steeply from k = 1 and levels off around k = 100. Check the exact percentages in the figure.

  • Where are ALOHA's four cameras, and which of those views does your wrist and front pair lack?ACT, hardware section
    Answer

    One on each follower wrist, one at the front and one looking down from the top. You lack the top-down view and the second wrist, since ALOHA is bimanual.

  • What does lerobot-record save for each frame, and what happens between episodes?LeRobot IL docs
    Answer

    Each camera image, the follower's joints as observation.state, the leader's as action, plus timestamp, indices and the task string. Images are encoded to MP4 during or after each episode. Between episodes a reset period (reset_time_s) runs unrecorded while you put the scene back. → ends an episode early, ← re-records it, Esc stops.

  • Given all of the above, write three rules for how you'll record your 50 demos.your own notes
    Answer

    One version. Freeze the scene (tape marks, lighting, patch, block) from demo 1 to the last evaluation trial. Do the task one way, smoothly and without pausing, because hesitations and mixed strategies are exactly what z and chunking have to absorb. Spread the block's start positions over a grid inside the reach, and start every episode from the same rest pose.

Build it yourself

Compounding error and chunking, in one dimension

Setup
A point moves along a line. The expert path x*(t) is a sine wave over 200 steps, and the expert's action at any state is the step that lands back on the path. Your policy is the expert plus an error each time it's queried: a small fixed bias plus Gaussian noise.
Three policies
Query every step. Query every k steps and play the k planned actions without looking (open loop). Query every step and combine all the chunks that overlap the current step, using the weighting from the paper.
Controls
Sliders for bias, noise, k and the weighting constant. A button that pushes the point off the path at step 100.
Plots
The three rollouts against x*(t), and final drift against k.
Predict first
Before you run it, write down what happens to drift as k grows, which version recovers fastest from the push, and what combining chunks does to jitter.
Check against
The method section and the chunk-size ablation. Where your toy disagrees with the paper, find the assumption that differs.

Answer in your own words
  • What does multimodal mean for actions, and why does a regression loss turn two good demos into one bad one?Diffusion Policy, introduction
    Answer

    Several different actions are right for the same observation, like passing an obstacle on the left or on the right. Mean squared error is minimised by the average of the targets, so the model predicts the midpoint of left and right: straight into the obstacle.

  • ACT faces the same problem. What does it use to cope, instead of diffusion?compare ACT's method with Diffusion Policy's
    Answer

    The CVAE latent z, which lets a demo's choice be encoded rather than averaged away, and chunking, which commits to one mode for a whole chunk. With z = 0 at test time it settles on one style instead of sampling between modes.

  • What does π0 use flow matching for, and what is its action expert attached to?π0, model section
    Answer

    To generate continuous action chunks: it learns a velocity field that carries noise to actions, integrated in a few steps at inference. The action expert is a smaller set of transformer weights attached to a pretrained vision-language model (PaliGemma), attending to its image and language tokens.

  • What does SmolVLA give up to run on a laptop, and what problem does async inference solve?tutorial §4.4 and §5.4
    Answer

    Size: a small VLM with fewer visual tokens per frame and only part of its layers, about 450M parameters against π0's 3.3B, trained on community datasets. Async inference computes the next chunk while the arm is still playing the current one, so there's no pause at chunk boundaries.

    Answer

Electronics and the bus

Bus
One set of shared wires that several devices hang off. A servo bus has three: ground, power and data. Every device on it sees every message.black = GND, red = V+, white = data. The F bus carries 12 V and the L bus 5 V.
Daisy chain
Wiring each device to the next one instead of each back to a hub. It works here because the two sockets on each servo are joined inside the case, so power and data pass straight through. Order and socket choice make no difference electrically.board → shoulder_pan → shoulder_lift → elbow_flex → wrist_flex → wrist_roll → gripper. The board joins at the base because shoulder_pan's body is the only motor bolted to the base, so that cable never flexes.
Motor ID
The address a servo answers to, a number from 0 to 253 stored in its EEPROM. Every new servo ships as ID 1.1 shoulder_pan, 2 shoulder_lift, 3 elbow_flex, 4 wrist_flex, 5 wrist_roll, 6 gripper, on both arms.
Broadcast ID
ID 254. A packet sent to it is acted on by every motor. A broadcast ping is how setup finds the single connected motor without knowing its ID yet.Try it in the packet simulator
Half-duplex serial
Two-way communication over one data wire, one side talking at a time. The board sends a command, then goes quiet and listens for the reply. If two motors reply at once their signals overlap and the board reads noise.
UART / serial
The simplest way two chips exchange bytes: one bit after another at an agreed speed, no clock wire. "TTL" means the signal swings between 0 V and the logic voltage (3.3 or 5 V), as opposed to the older ±12 V RS-232.
Baud rate
Symbols per second on the serial line, which for UART equals bits per second. Both ends have to use the same rate or the bytes come out as garbage.1,000,000 baud. lerobot-setup-motors writes the baud rate as well as the ID.
Packet
One framed message on the bus: a header, the target ID, a length, an instruction (ping, read, write, sync write), parameters, and a checksum so the receiver can spot corruption.
Status packet
The reply a motor sends back. LeRobot's "Incorrect status packet" warning means a reply arrived corrupted, usually from a loose cable, electrical noise or a sagging supply.
Sync read / sync write
One packet that reads or writes the same register on many motors at once. LeRobot reads all six positions this way every loop, which is far faster than six separate requests.
Bus servo adapter
What the kit calls the controller board. It contains a USB-to-serial chip, a small circuit that merges transmit and receive onto the single data wire, and a pass-through that sends the brick's power to the servo socket. It runs no program of yours.one board per arm, labelled F and L.
Microcontroller (MCU)
A small computer on one chip: processor, memory and input/output pins, running one program in a loop. Arduino, ESP32 and RP2040 boards are built around one. Your adapter board doesn't have one you program, but every STS3215 has one inside. That's what runs the motor's position loop and answers packets.
Serial port
The operating system's name for a serial device. On a Mac, USB serial devices appear as /dev/tty.usbmodem…, with the suffix taken from the chip's serial number, so each board keeps its name across replugs.F = …5B7B0168461, L = …5B7B0093921, saved in so101/ports.sh.
Jumper
A small cap that bridges two header pins to select a circuit path.on the adapter the jumpers choose where the serial line goes. Position B connects it to USB, which is what you want. A connects it to header pins for an external microcontroller.
Screw terminal
A clamp for bare wire: loosen the screw, insert the stripped end, tighten. Polarity matters for DC power.
Voltage and current
Voltage is electrical pressure and has to match what the motor is built for. Current (amps) is how much flows. A supply's amp rating is a ceiling on current, and the motors only draw what they need.12 V on the follower, 5 V on the leader (7.4 V motors run slower and weaker at 5 V, which suits a leader). 12 V into the leader motors burns them.
Ground (GND)
The shared 0 V reference that every voltage is measured from. Devices that talk to each other have to share it.
Brownout
The supply voltage sags under a heavy draw and electronics reset or misread. Classic symptom: serial errors that only happen while the arm moves.the follower ran on a 2.0 A brick until 14 Nov, against the 5 A the BOM asks for, and fast moves lagged on it. The 5 A brick holds 12.1–12.3 V at every motor.

Motors

Reflected inertia
What the rotor's inertia feels like from the horn: multiplied by the square of the gear ratio. It's why a 1:345 motor feels heavy to back-drive and a 1:147 doesn't.Chapter 1
Static holding torque
The torque a joint must supply to hold a pose against gravity, before any acceleration. It depends only on the masses and where they sit relative to that joint's axis.The load bars under the 3D arm
Servo
A motor, gearbox, position sensor and controller in one case. You send it a target position and it drives itself there and holds.
Serial bus servo
A servo controlled by addressed packets on a shared bus, instead of a separate PWM signal wire per motor as on hobby servos. It can also report back its position, load, voltage and temperature.
STS3215
The Feetech serial bus servo used throughout the SO-100 and SO-101, available with several gear ratios and supply voltages.follower 6 × STS3215-12V (C047, 1:345). Leader C001 1:345 ×1, C044 1:191 ×2, C046 1:147 ×3, all 7.4 V.
Gear ratio
Motor revolutions per output revolution. A higher ratio gives more torque and holding force, but the output turns slower and is harder to push by hand.the leader uses 1:345 on shoulder_lift to hold up the arm's weight, 1:191 on shoulder_pan and elbow_flex, and 1:147 on the wrist and gripper so they're light under your fingers.
Torque
Turning force. Servo torque is usually quoted in kg·cm: 30 kg·cm means it can hold 30 kg at 1 cm from the shaft, or 1 kg at 30 cm. Longer reach means a smaller load.the follower's 12 V motors are rated at about 30 kg·cm.
Stall
The motor pushing without moving, for example a gripper squeezing a block. This is when current and heat peak.LeRobot limits the follower gripper to 50% torque and 50% protection current because it stalls on every grasp.
Backdrivable
Turnable from the output side by an outside force. A leader arm has to be backdrivable because you move it.
Horn
The disc on the servo's output shaft that the printed part screws onto.
Spline
The toothed output shaft the horn presses onto. The teeth transmit the torque.the silent, free-swinging shoulder joint during the leader build was a horn not seated on its spline.
Encoder
A sensor that reports shaft angle. The STS3215 has a magnetic encoder on the output shaft giving 4096 counts per turn, about 0.088° per count. It's absolute within one turn, so it knows its position at power-on without homing.
Tick / raw position
One encoder count. Raw positions run 0–4095.
Register
A numbered memory slot in the servo that you read or write over the bus: ID, Goal_Position, Present_Position, Torque_Enable, P_Coefficient and so on. The whole list is the control table.The register map
EEPROM vs RAM
EEPROM keeps its contents through power-off and holds settings: ID, baud rate, limits, homing offset. RAM resets at power-off and holds live values: goal position, torque on or off. A Lock register guards EEPROM against accidental writes, and EEPROM wears out after a limited number of writes.Guess which is which in the register map
Torque enable
On: the motor drives to and holds its goal. Off: the motor goes limp and can be turned by hand, but still reports its position.the leader runs with torque off, and the follower with torque on.
Goal vs present position
Goal is where you told the motor to go, and present is where the encoder says it is. The gap between them is the error the control loop works to remove.
Overload protection
The servo cuts or reduces its own torque when current or load stays too high for too long. It protects the motor, but it can also look like a joint suddenly going weak.
Status LED
The red light on the servo. On a Feetech, steady means powered, and blinking means an alarm such as overload, overheating or a voltage outside its range.

Mechanics and kinematics

Link
A rigid piece between two joints: upper arm, forearm, wrist.
Joint
Where two links meet and move relative to each other. A revolute joint rotates and a prismatic joint slides.all six joints are revolute.
Actuated vs passive
An actuated joint has a motor driving it. A passive joint moves freely or is driven by something else, like a caster wheel or a sprung hinge.
End effector
Whatever is on the end of the arm doing the job: gripper, suction cup, pen, camera.
Gripper
A 1-DOF end effector that opens and closes.the follower has one fixed jaw and one jaw driven by motor 6. On the leader, the trigger is screwed to motor 6's horn.
Degrees of freedom (DOF)
The number of independent values needed to pin down a configuration. For a serial arm that's one per independent joint. "Degrees of motion" isn't a standard term; DOF is the one to use.6 actuated DOF: 5 that place and orient the hand, plus 1 for the gripper. Sellers call it "6-DOF"; roboticists would say "5-DOF arm with a gripper".The 3D arm · The counting sim
Pose
Position plus orientation. A rigid object in 3D has a 6-DOF pose: x, y, z, and three rotations (roll, pitch, yaw). An arm needs at least 6 DOF to reach every pose inside its workspace.with 5 arm DOF the SO-101 can't set all six independently: the approach direction always lies in the plane set by shoulder_pan.
Joint space / configuration space
The space of all joint-angle combinations, one axis per joint. Your arm's configuration is a single point in a 6-dimensional box. Also called C-space.
Task space / Cartesian space
The space where the job is described: where the gripper is and which way it points, in millimetres and angles.
Forward kinematics (FK)
Joint angles in, hand pose out. It's a chain of rotations and translations, so there's always exactly one answer.The readout under the 3D arm is FK
Inverse kinematics (IK)
Desired hand pose in, joint angles out. There can be no solution (out of reach), a few (elbow up or down), or infinitely many (redundant arm). Usually solved numerically by iterating with the Jacobian.The redundancy sim
Workspace
Every point the hand can reach. The dexterous workspace is the smaller region it can reach in any orientation.
Redundancy
More DOF than the task needs. The spare freedom can be spent on avoiding obstacles, staying away from joint limits, or keeping the elbow clear. 7-DOF arms like the Franka, or your own arm from shoulder to wrist, are redundant for 6-DOF poses.
Null space
The joint motions that leave the hand exactly where it is. It exists only when the arm is redundant.Sweep φ in the redundancy sim
Jacobian
The matrix that turns joint velocities into hand velocity, ẋ = J(q) q̇. It changes with the pose. IK solvers invert it, or its pseudo-inverse, step by step.
Singularity
A pose where the Jacobian loses rank and the hand can't move in some direction, for example with the arm stretched fully straight. Near one, IK asks for huge joint speeds.
Joint limits / range of motion
How far each joint can turn before it hits the printed parts or a software limit. Calibration records them.
DH parameters
Denavit–Hartenberg: a classic convention describing each joint with four numbers (link length, twist, offset, angle) so FK becomes a product of matrices.
URDF / MJCF
XML files that describe a robot's links, joints, masses, limits and meshes. URDF is the ROS format and MJCF is MuJoCo's. The SO-101 has both, and your sim-twin idea starts from one of them.

Control

Sag
The steady gap between goal and position when gravity loads a joint and the controller has no integral term. It's the gravity torque divided by the proportional gain, and it shows up in your data as the difference between action and observation.state.The teleop sim
Control loop
Measure, compare with the target, command, repeat. Closed loop uses the measurement; open loop sends commands blind.
Setpoint and error
The setpoint is the target value. Error is setpoint minus measurement.
PID
The standard feedback controller: u = Kₚe + Kᵢ∫e dt + K_d de/dt. P pushes in proportion to the error, I wipes out a small error that won't go away, and D damps the motion by reacting to how fast the error is changing. Too much P overshoots and oscillates; too much D makes it sluggish and noisy.this runs inside each STS3215. LeRobot writes P = 16, I = 0, D = 32 to the follower when it connects, softening P to cut jitter.
Gains
The constants Kₚ, Kᵢ, K_d. Tuning a controller means choosing them.
Overshoot, oscillation, steady-state error
Overshoot: going past the target. Oscillation: swinging back and forth around it. Steady-state error: settling a little short of it.
Operating mode
What the servo treats as its command: position (mode 0, what LeRobot uses), speed, or raw PWM power.
Control frequency
How often the outer loop runs, in Hz. The servo's internal loop runs much faster than that.lerobot-teleoperate defaults to 60 Hz: read leader, write follower, 60 times a second.
Latency
The delay between a command and the motion it causes, or between an event and the camera frame that shows it. Policies trained on data at one latency can struggle at another.
max_relative_target
A LeRobot follower setting that caps how far any one command may move a joint from its current position. Useful as a guardrail while you're still trusting the setup.
Compliance
How much the arm gives when pushed. Low P gain or torque off means compliant, and high gain means stiff. Contact-rich tasks want some.
Trajectory
A path through joint or task space over time, positions with timestamps. ELEGNT's τ.

Calibration and LeRobot

Calibration
Mapping each motor's raw ticks to meaningful joint values, consistently across both arms, so that "elbow at 30°" means the same pose on leader and follower.
Homing offset
A value written to each motor so that the middle of its range reads 2047, half a turn. This is why calibration starts with "move to the middle of its range of motion".The encoder seam sim
Range recording
The second calibration step. You sweep every joint end to end while LeRobot records the minimum and maximum it sees.wrist_roll is skipped and treated as a full turn, 0–4095.
Normalisation
How LeRobot presents joint values after calibration: degrees for the arm joints and 0–100 for the gripper, the same on both arms.
Calibration file
A JSON file of offsets and ranges, saved under the arm's id in ~/.cache/huggingface/lerobot/calibration/. Use the same --robot.id / --teleop.id every time, or LeRobot won't find it.
Robot vs teleoperator
LeRobot's two device roles. A robot is something that acts (so101_follower, with cameras) and a teleoperator is something a human uses to command it (so101_leader, a keyboard, a gamepad).
MotorsBus
LeRobot's object for one serial port plus the motors on it. In 0.7.0 it's SerialMotorsBus, driving the motors through the Rust library rustypot; the old FeetechMotorsBus name still works with a deprecation warning. Your scripts use it directly.
Rerun
A viewer for time-series and camera streams. --display_data=true pipes joint values and frames into it live.included in the repo's core-scripts extra.
Hugging Face Hub
Where LeRobot datasets and trained policies get pushed and pulled, by repo id such as user/so101_pick.
Encoder seam
Where the raw count wraps from 4095 back to 0. If a joint's travel crosses it, range recording sees both 0 and 4095 and records nearly a full turn. Half-turn homing puts the held pose at 2047, opposite the seam, so the travel fits as long as the pose is near the middle.failure 5: elbow range 1–4095 after setting the mid pose with the arm folded. Now 82° clear of the seam.The encoder seam sim
read_latest
How the follower reads its cameras in each teleop loop: it takes the newest frame without waiting for a fresh one, so 30 fps cameras don't slow a 60 Hz loop. A frame older than 0.5 s stops teleop with a TimeoutError.

Cameras

UVC
USB Video Class, the standard webcam protocol. Any UVC camera works without a driver, and OpenCV opens it by index.the ET-S231 is a bare 32 × 32 mm UVC board.
Field of view (FOV)
How wide the camera sees. Wide lenses bend straight lines near the edges (barrel or fisheye distortion).120°: wide enough to see the gripper and the table from close range.
Manual focus
Focus set by twisting the lens in its threaded mount. Set it once at the working distance and leave it.
Wrist camera vs scene camera
A wrist camera rides on the gripper and sees the grasp up close. A scene camera is fixed and sees the whole workspace. Many SO-101 policies use one of each.
uniqueID
macOS's identifier for a capture device. A USB camera's is a hex string built from the port it's in and its vendor and product IDs, so moving a camera to another port can change the order. Built-in and iPhone cameras get a UUID.wrist 0x13000029930858, Insta360 0x1200002e1a1003, so the Insta360 sorts first.
Continuity Camera
macOS using a nearby iPhone as a webcam. The phone appears as one or two extra cameras (main and Desk View) while it's in range, and disappears when it isn't.the reason the wrist index opened the MacBook camera on 29 Oct (failure 20).
Default vs requested mode
A camera offers several combinations of size, frame rate and pixel format. lerobot-find-cameras opens each camera in its default. Teleop and record ask for the mode in --robot.cameras, and LeRobot stops with an error if the camera refuses it or sends frames of a different size.check_camera.py opens the requested mode and times the frames.
Exposure and frame rate
In dim light a webcam's auto exposure holds each frame open longer, and a frame can't be shorter than its exposure, so the frame rate falls, often from 30 to 15. Add light before blaming the camera.
Aspect ratio
Width over height: 4:3 (640 × 480) or 16:9 (640 × 360, 1280 × 720). Asking a 16:9 camera for 4:3 either fails or returns a squashed picture, depending on the driver.wrist 640 × 480 at 30.3 fps, Insta360 640 × 360 at 29.9 fps.
Image rotation
A camera setting (rotation: 90 | 180 | -90) that turns frames after capture. Width and height in the config are the size after rotation, so a 640 × 480 camera turned 90° is configured as 480 × 640.wrist left at 0. The jaws enter from the right of the frame, and the view is fixed once the mount is bolted.
Camera index
The number OpenCV gives each camera (0, 1, 2…). On macOS it's the camera's position after sorting every camera's uniqueID as text, so plugging in, unplugging, or an iPhone coming into range can renumber everything. lerobot-find-cameras opencv lists them and saves a frame from each.Insta360 0, wrist 1, FaceTime 2 with all three connected. camera_index.py --export sets WRIST_CAM and FRONT_CAM by name.

Learning from demonstrations

Token
One element of the sequence a transformer attends over. In ACT that's one for z, one for the state, and one for each 32 × 32 patch of each camera: 542 on your rig.The ACT explorer
Teleoperation
Driving a robot live from a human input device.
Leader–follower
Teleop with a kinematically matching arm: the follower copies the leader's joint angles directly, so no IK is needed. ALOHA popularised it for learning.
Episode / demonstration
One recorded attempt at the task from start to finish, for example one pick-and-place.
Observation, state, action
Observation is what the policy sees at a timestep: camera frames plus the follower's joint state. Action is what it outputs. In leader–follower data the action is the leader's joint positions at that moment.
LeRobotDataset
LeRobot's dataset format: joint data and metadata in Parquet files, each camera stream as MP4 video, indexed by episode and frame.
Policy
The learned function from observations to actions, usually a neural network.
Imitation learning / behaviour cloning
Training a policy with supervised learning to copy demonstrated actions. Behaviour cloning is the simplest form.
Compounding error
Small mistakes put the robot in states the demonstrations never covered, which leads to bigger mistakes. It's the central weakness of behaviour cloning, and the reason demo diversity matters.
ACT
Action Chunking with Transformers (Zhao et al., 2023, from the ALOHA work). A transformer, trained as a CVAE, predicts a chunk of future actions from images and joint state. LeRobot's standard first policy for the SO-101.
Action chunk
A short sequence of future actions predicted in one go. It smooths motion and reduces compounding error compared with predicting one step at a time.The ACT explorer · your 1-D toy
Diffusion Policy
A policy that generates an action chunk by denoising random noise, the same way image diffusion models work. Good at multimodal demos (two valid ways to do something).
VLA
Vision-language-action model: a pretrained vision-language model fine-tuned to output robot actions, so it can take an instruction in words. SmolVLA and π0 are both in LeRobot.
Rollout / evaluation
Running the trained policy on the real arm and counting successes over a fixed number of trials. Success rate is the headline metric.
Distribution shift
Test conditions differing from training: new lighting, a moved camera, a different block. Policies trained on a few dozen demos are very sensitive to it.
Sim-to-real
Training in simulation and deploying on hardware. Domain randomisation, varying textures, masses and lighting in sim, is the usual way across the gap.
World model
A learned model that predicts how the world will change given an action. Used for planning, or for training policies inside the model instead of on the robot.
